Polynomials

The CKKS scheme works over polynomials in rings of the form $\bigl(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\bigr)[X]/(X^N + 1)$. To make everything concrete, we'll pin down specific numbers throughout this part. Let $N = 2^{16} = 65536$ be the ring dimension, and we fix a chain of distinct primes $q_0, q_1, \ldots, q_{15}$, all congruent to $1$ modulo $2^{17} = 131072$, with $q_0 \approx 2^{55}$ and $q_1 \approx q_2 \approx \cdots \approx q_{15} \approx 2^{40}$. Throughout, we'll write

$$ R_\infty = \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^{65536} + 1), \qquad R_\ell = \bigl(\mathbb{Z}/(q_0 q_1 \cdots q_\ell)\mathbb{Z}\bigr)[X]/(X^{65536} + 1), $$

so $R_\ell$ is the polynomial ring at level $\ell \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 15\}$, and $R_\infty$ is the underlying integer-coefficient ring.

Everything in CKKS is built on top of two representations of the polynomials in $R_\ell$ and ten operations on them, each introduced in its own subsection below.

Representations

A polynomial in $R_\ell$ has two equivalent representations: the coefficient domain, which stores its $65536$ integer coefficients, and the evaluation domain, which stores its values at a fixed set of $65536$ primitive $131072$-th roots of unity. Most of the operations below can be performed in one or both of these representations.

Number-theoretic transform

The number-theoretic transform (NTT) and its inverse (iNTT) convert between the coefficient and evaluation domains.

[WORK IN PROGRESS]

Addition and subtraction

Can be performed in either representation.

[WORK IN PROGRESS]

Scalar multiplication

Multiplying every coefficient (or every evaluation) by a constant. Can be performed in either representation. Multiplication by a polynomial of the form $a + b X^{32768}$ is similarly efficient.

[WORK IN PROGRESS]

Polynomial multiplication

Performed in the evaluation domain only.

[WORK IN PROGRESS]

Automorphism

Can be performed in either representation.

[WORK IN PROGRESS]

Rescale

Performed in the coefficient domain only.

[WORK IN PROGRESS]

Modulus reduction

Given a level $\ell' < \ell$ (so that $q' = q_0 \cdots q_{\ell'}$ divides $q = q_0 \cdots q_\ell$), reduce a polynomial in $R_\ell$ to one in $R_{\ell'}$ by taking each coefficient modulo $q'$. Coefficient domain only.

[WORK IN PROGRESS]

Modraise

Performed in the coefficient domain only.
