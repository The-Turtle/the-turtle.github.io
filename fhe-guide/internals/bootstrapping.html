Bootstrapping

Once a ciphertext reaches level $0$, no further multiplications using it are possible. Bootstrapping is the operation that "refreshes" a level-$0$ ciphertext, returning a level-$17$ ciphertext encrypting the same vector.

Bootstrapping consists of four steps:

  1. Mod-raise. Reinterpret the level-$0$ ciphertext as a ciphertext at level $30$. The underlying plaintext polynomial picks up a multiple of $q_0$ in each coefficient.
  2. Coefficients-to-slots. Use a pair of homomorphic linear transforms to package the $N = 65536$ coefficients of the (now corrupted) plaintext polynomial into the $N/2 = 32768$ slots of two ciphertexts.
  3. Mod-eval. Evaluate a polynomial approximation of $x \bmod q_0$ slot-wise to strip off the multiple of $q_0$ introduced in step 1.
  4. Slots-to-coefficients. Reverse the packing of step 2: a pair of homomorphic linear transforms turns the two slot-packed ciphertexts back into a single ciphertext whose plaintext polynomial holds the recovered coefficients.

Before any of this can happen, the modulus chain needs to be extended with new primes (to give the bootstrap room to operate), and the key set needs to be augmented with two new key-switching keys built around an additional secret key. We'll set these up first, then walk through each of the four steps.

Extending the modulus chain

The bootstrap consumes $13$ levels in total, so the modulus chain is extended with $13$ distinct primes $q_{18}, q_{19}, \ldots, q_{30}$, each approximately $q_0 \approx 2^{55}$ and congruent to $1$ modulo $2^{17}$. The extended chain has $31$ levels in total, and each new level has a scale factor around $2^{55}$ given by the recurrence

$$ \Delta_{30} = 2^{55}, \qquad \Delta_{\ell - 1} = \frac{\Delta_\ell^{\,2}}{q_\ell} \quad\text{for } \ell \in \{19, \ldots, 30\}. $$

The $13$ new levels are budgeted as $3$ levels for coefficients-to-slots, $7$ levels for mod-eval, and $3$ levels for slots-to-coefficients.

Remark. The recurrence holds within each piece of the chain — $\ell \in \{19, \ldots, 30\}$ and $\ell \in \{1, \ldots, 17\}$ — but it breaks at the seam between them. If the recurrence were extended to $\ell = 18$, it would predict $\Delta_{17}^{\,\text{predicted}} \approx \Delta_{18}^{\,2} / q_{18} \approx 2^{55}$, but the chain actually has $\Delta_{17}^{\,\text{actual}} = 2^{40}$ from Section 3.2. The consequence is that a multiplication that takes a level-$18$ ciphertext down to level $17$ picks up an extra scaling factor of

$$ \frac{\Delta_{17}^{\,\text{actual}}}{\Delta_{17}^{\,\text{predicted}}} \;=\; 2^{-15}. $$

Sparse secret encapsulation

Alongside the regular keys, generate:

  1. an ephemeral sparse secret $s' \in R_\infty$ with coefficients in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and exactly $h' = 32$ nonzero coefficients, chosen uniformly at random from all such polynomials;
  2. a public key-switching key $\mathrm{ksk}_{s \to s'}$, generated at level $0$ only;
  3. a public key-switching key $\mathrm{ksk}_{s' \to s}$, generated at level $30$, in similar fashion.

Both key-switching keys follow the same blueprint as the key-switching key in Section 3.3: $\mathrm{ksk}_{s \to s'}$ is the analogue of $\mathrm{ksk}_s$ from that section but with the encrypting key $s$ replaced by $s'$ and only the level-$0$ block populated, while $\mathrm{ksk}_{s' \to s}$ is exactly $\mathrm{ksk}_{s'}$ from that section (with the encrypting key $s$ as usual), constructed at the full level $30$.

Once both $\mathrm{ksk}_{s \to s'}$ and $\mathrm{ksk}_{s' \to s}$ are built, the polynomial $s'$ itself can be discarded.

Mod-raise

The mod-raise step takes a level-$0$ ciphertext $(c_0, c_1) \in R_0 \times R_0$ encrypting a plaintext polynomial $m \in R_0$ and returns a level-$30$ ciphertext encrypting $m + q_0\, t$ for some $t \in R_\infty$ with small coefficients. Internally, it does this in three sub-steps:

  1. Apply $\mathrm{ksk}_{s \to s'}$ to $(c_0, c_1)$ to obtain a level-$0$ ciphertext $(c_0', c_1') \in R_0 \times R_0$ satisfying $c_0' + c_1'\, s' \approx m$.
  2. Apply modulus raising to $c_0'$ and $c_1'$ with target level $30$ to get $$ c_0'' \;=\; \underset{q_0 \,\to\, q_0 \cdots q_{30}}{\mathrm{modraise}}(c_0'), \qquad c_1'' \;=\; \underset{q_0 \,\to\, q_0 \cdots q_{30}}{\mathrm{modraise}}(c_1'). $$ Now, $c_0'' + c_1''\, s' \approx m + q_0\, t$ for some $t \in R_\infty$.
  3. Apply $\mathrm{ksk}_{s' \to s}$ to $(c_0'', c_1'')$ to obtain a level-$30$ ciphertext encrypting $m + q_0\, t$.

The distribution of $t$

The coefficients of $c_0''$ and $c_1''$ are the centered lifts of the coefficients of $c_0'$ and $c_1'$, which are indistinguishable from uniform draws on $\bigl\{-\tfrac{q_0 - 1}{2},\, \ldots,\, \tfrac{q_0 - 1}{2}\bigr\}$ by the RLWE assumption. Writing $c_1'' = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} c_i\, X^i$, the product $c_1''\, s'$ in $R_{30}$ expands degree by degree as

$$ c_1''\, s' \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\, \biggl(\sum_{j \in \mathrm{supp}(s')} (c_1'')_{[k - j]}\biggr) X^k, $$

where $(c_1'')_{[k - j]}$ is the appropriately-signed coefficient of $c_1''$ that arises from multiplying the $j$-th monomial of $s'$ through: it equals $\pm c_{k - j}$ if $k \ge j$ and $\mp c_{k + N - j}$ otherwise, with the sign determined by $s'_j \in \{-1, +1\}$ and the negacyclic relation $X^N = -1$ in $R_{30}$. The outer sum is over the $N$ degrees, and the inner sum is over the $h' = 32$ positions at which $s'$ is nonzero. So every coefficient of $c_1''\, s'$ is a signed sum of $h' = 32$ independent uniform draws on $\bigl\{-\tfrac{q_0 - 1}{2},\, \ldots,\, \tfrac{q_0 - 1}{2}\bigr\}$ — which, since that set is symmetric about $0$, is the same distribution as a sum of $h' = 32$ unsigned uniform draws on it. Adding $c_0''$ contributes one more independent uniform draw.

The relation $c_0'' + c_1''\, s' \approx m + q_0\, t$ in $R_{30}$ gives

$$ t \;\approx\; \frac{c_0'' + c_1''\, s' - m}{q_0} \;\in\; R_\infty, $$

where $c_0''$ and $c_1''$ are interpreted as polynomials in $R_\infty$. For typical messages the coefficients of $m$ are much smaller than $q_0/2$ (with $|m_i| \lesssim \Delta_0 \approx 2^{40}$ for unit-magnitude encrypted vectors, versus $q_0/2 \approx 2^{54}$), so the $-m$ shift in the numerator is negligible. To excellent approximation, each coefficient of $t$ is then distributed as

$$ t_i \;\sim\; \sum_{k=1}^{h' + 1} U_k, \qquad U_k \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathrm{Uniform}\!\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2},\, \tfrac{1}{2}\bigr). $$

This is the centered Irwin–Hall distribution with parameter $h' + 1 = 33$. Its support is $[-(h'+1)/2,\, (h'+1)/2] = [-16.5,\, 16.5]$, its mean is $0$, and its standard deviation is $\sqrt{(h'+1)/12} \approx 1.66$.

Coefficients-to-slots

At this point, the mod-raise has produced a level-$30$ ciphertext under $s$ whose plaintext polynomial is

$$ m^\star \;\approx\; m + q_0\, t, $$

with $\|t\|_\infty \le K = 16$. We'd like to strip off the $q_0\, t$ term, but the only operation CKKS gives us that acts non-linearly on the underlying plaintext is slot-wise polynomial evaluation. So we first need to move the $N = 65536$ coefficients of $m^\star$ into the slots of some ciphertexts.

Recall from Section 3.2 that encoding a vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{C}^{32768}$ as a plaintext polynomial $m^\star$ at scale $\Delta$ chooses $m^\star$ so that $m^\star(\zeta^{5^j}) = \Delta\, v_j$ for $j = 0, 1, \ldots, 32767$, where $\zeta = e^{2\pi i / 131072}$. Equivalently, $m^\star$ has the $32768$ values $m^\star(\zeta),\, m^\star(\zeta^5),\, \ldots,\, m^\star(\zeta^{5^{32767}})$ in its slots.

Coefficients-to-slots produces two level-$27$ ciphertexts that together hold the $N = 65536$ coefficients of $m^\star$, in bit-reversed order, in their $2 \cdot 32768$ slots:

$$ \mathrm{slots}(\,\boxed{\,\cdot\,}_1\,) = \bigl(m_{\mathrm{rev}(0)},\;\; m_{\mathrm{rev}(2)},\;\; m_{\mathrm{rev}(4)},\;\; \ldots,\;\; m_{\mathrm{rev}(N-2)}\bigr), $$ $$ \mathrm{slots}(\,\boxed{\,\cdot\,}_2\,) = \bigl(m_{\mathrm{rev}(1)},\;\; m_{\mathrm{rev}(3)},\;\; m_{\mathrm{rev}(5)},\;\; \ldots,\;\; m_{\mathrm{rev}(N-1)}\bigr), $$

where $m_k$ is the $k$th coefficient of $m^\star$ and $\mathrm{rev}(k)$ denotes the bit-reversal of $k$ as a $\log_2 N = 16$-bit binary number. The bit reversal is a quirk of the matrix factorization we'll see below; it doesn't matter beyond the bookkeeping, because the inverse step in three subsections puts everything back in order.

The two matrices

The map from coefficients to slots boils down to two $(N/2) \times (N/2)$ matrix–vector multiplications. To see how, fix the slot index structure $\bigl(m^\star(\zeta),\, m^\star(\zeta^5),\, \ldots,\, m^\star(\zeta^{5^{N/2 - 1}})\bigr)$, and observe that for any polynomial of degree less than $N$, $\sum_{k} m_k\, \zeta^{5^j\, k}$ is a sum of $N$ terms, with $N/2$ "even-$k$" terms involving $\zeta^2$ to various powers and $N/2$ "odd-$k$" terms with an extra factor of $\zeta^{5^j}$. After some algebra (worked out in the docstring of coefficients_to_slots in the reference implementation), and using the bit-reversal trick to arrange the rows so the upcoming sparse factorization is clean, the even-coefficient ciphertext $\boxed{\,\cdot\,}_1$ comes out as $N$ times

$$ L\, \begin{pmatrix} m^\star(\zeta) \\ m^\star(\zeta^5) \\ \vdots \\ m^\star(\zeta^{5^{N/2 - 1}}) \end{pmatrix} \;+\; \overline{L}\, \begin{pmatrix} m^\star(\zeta^{-1}) \\ m^\star(\zeta^{-5}) \\ \vdots \\ m^\star(\zeta^{-5^{N/2 - 1}}) \end{pmatrix} $$

and the odd-coefficient ciphertext $\boxed{\,\cdot\,}_2$ comes out as $N$ times

$$ (-i)\cdot L'\, \begin{pmatrix} m^\star(\zeta) \\ m^\star(\zeta^5) \\ \vdots \\ m^\star(\zeta^{5^{N/2 - 1}}) \end{pmatrix} \;+\; (\,i\,)\cdot \overline{L'}\, \begin{pmatrix} m^\star(\zeta^{-1}) \\ m^\star(\zeta^{-5}) \\ \vdots \\ m^\star(\zeta^{-5^{N/2 - 1}}) \end{pmatrix}, $$

where $L$ is the $(N/2) \times (N/2)$ matrix

$$ L \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\ \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(2)} & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(2)\cdot 5} & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(2)\cdot 25} & \cdots & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(2)\cdot 5^{N/2 - 1}} \\ \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(4)} & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(4)\cdot 5} & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(4)\cdot 25} & \cdots & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(4)\cdot 5^{N/2 - 1}} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(N-2)} & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(N-2)\cdot 5} & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(N-2)\cdot 25} & \cdots & \zeta^{-\mathrm{rev}(N-2)\cdot 5^{N/2 - 1}} \end{pmatrix} $$

and $L'$ is the same matrix with odd indices $\mathrm{rev}(1), \mathrm{rev}(3), \ldots, \mathrm{rev}(N-1)$ on the rows instead of even ones. The conjugate matrices $\overline{L}$ and $\overline{L'}$ are obtained slot-wise from $L$ and $L'$ using the conjugation operation from Section 3.3: $\bigl(m^\star(\zeta^{-1}), \ldots\bigr)$ is just the complex conjugate of $\bigl(m^\star(\zeta), \ldots\bigr)$ in the slots, and applying $\overline{L}$ to the conjugated vector gives the conjugate of $L$ times the original vector.

A small additional observation cuts the work in half again: the matrix $L'$ turns out to be $L$ multiplied slot-wise by the vector $(\zeta^{-1}, \zeta^{-5}, \ldots, \zeta^{-5^{N/2-1}})$, and that vector is just the slot representation of the polynomial $X$. So $L'$ never has to be applied directly: compute the single product $L\, \boxed{m^\star}$, and the two output ciphertexts are

$$ \boxed{\,\cdot\,}_1 \;=\; L\,\boxed{m^\star} + \overline{L\,\boxed{m^\star}}, \qquad \boxed{\,\cdot\,}_2 \;=\; \bigl(\,\overline{L\,\boxed{m^\star}} - L\,\boxed{m^\star}\,\bigr) \cdot i, $$

where each overline denotes one application of the conjugation operation from Section 3.3. So all the heavy lifting is in computing one matrix–ciphertext product $L\, \boxed{m^\star}$.

Sparse factorization of $L$

Naive matrix–vector multiplication against $L$ via the algorithm of Section 3.4 needs to handle a matrix with $\Theta(N)$ nonzero diagonals, giving a runtime of order $\sqrt{N}$ rotations. We can do much better. The matrix $L$ factors as a product of $\log_2(N/2) = 15$ sparse matrices, each with just three nonzero diagonals. To illustrate, here is the factorization for the toy ring dimension $N = 16$ (so $L$ is $8 \times 8$). Naming the three sparse factors $A_2$, $A_1$, $A_0$ (in order of application, right-to-left),

$$ L \;=\; A_2 \cdot A_1 \cdot A_0, $$

where

$$ A_0 \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & & & & \zeta^{-1} & & & \\ & 1 & & & & \zeta^{-5} & & \\ & & 1 & & & & \zeta^{-25} & \\ & & & 1 & & & & \zeta^{-29} \\ \zeta^{-17} & & & & 1 & & & \\ & \zeta^{-21} & & & & 1 & & \\ & & \zeta^{-9} & & & & 1 & \\ & & & \zeta^{-13} & & & & 1 \end{pmatrix}, $$ $$ A_1 \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & & 1 & & & & & \\ & 1 & & 1 & & & & \\ \zeta^{-2} & & \zeta^{-18} & & & & & \\ & \zeta^{-10} & & \zeta^{-26} & & & & \\ & & & & 1 & & 1 & \\ & & & & & 1 & & 1 \\ & & & & \zeta^{-2} & & \zeta^{-18} & \\ & & & & & \zeta^{-10} & & \zeta^{-26} \end{pmatrix}, $$ $$ A_2 \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & & & & & & \\ \zeta^{-4} & \zeta^{-20} & & & & & & \\ & & 1 & 1 & & & & \\ & & \zeta^{-4} & \zeta^{-20} & & & & \\ & & & & 1 & 1 & & \\ & & & & \zeta^{-4} & \zeta^{-20} & & \\ & & & & & & 1 & 1 \\ & & & & & & \zeta^{-4} & \zeta^{-20} \end{pmatrix}. $$

Multiplying out $A_2 \cdot A_1 \cdot A_0$ recovers

$$ L \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ \zeta^{-4} & \zeta^{-20} & \zeta^{-4} & \zeta^{-20} & \zeta^{-4} & \zeta^{-20} & \zeta^{-4} & \zeta^{-20} \\ \zeta^{-2} & \zeta^{-10} & \zeta^{-18} & \zeta^{-26} & \zeta^{-2} & \zeta^{-10} & \zeta^{-18} & \zeta^{-26} \\ \zeta^{-6} & \zeta^{-30} & \zeta^{-22} & \zeta^{-14} & \zeta^{-6} & \zeta^{-30} & \zeta^{-22} & \zeta^{-14} \\ \zeta^{-1} & \zeta^{-5} & \zeta^{-25} & \zeta^{-29} & \zeta^{-17} & \zeta^{-21} & \zeta^{-9} & \zeta^{-13} \\ \zeta^{-5} & \zeta^{-25} & \zeta^{-29} & \zeta^{-17} & \zeta^{-21} & \zeta^{-9} & \zeta^{-13} & \zeta^{-1} \\ \zeta^{-3} & \zeta^{-15} & \zeta^{-11} & \zeta^{-23} & \zeta^{-19} & \zeta^{-31} & \zeta^{-27} & \zeta^{-7} \\ \zeta^{-7} & \zeta^{-3} & \zeta^{-15} & \zeta^{-11} & \zeta^{-23} & \zeta^{-19} & \zeta^{-31} & \zeta^{-27} \end{pmatrix}. $$

Each factor is a sparse matrix with exactly three nonzero diagonals (counting the main diagonal), and the diagonal offsets halve from one factor to the next: $4 \to 2 \to 1$ in the $N = 16$ example, and generally $N/4 \to N/8 \to \cdots \to 2 \to 1$ in the general case.

For $N = 65536$, the full factorization has $\log_2(N/2) = 15$ such sparse factors. Applying all $15$ individually would consume $15$ levels, far more than the $3$ we've budgeted — so we combine consecutive factors by ordinary matrix multiplication into $3$ stages, each somewhat denser than a single factor but consuming only one level. Concretely, group the $15$ factors into three contiguous blocks of $5$, multiply within each block, and then apply the three resulting matrices in sequence using the baby-step giant-step algorithm from Section 3.4.

The fewer stages we use, the fewer levels we consume but the denser each stage's matrix becomes; the three-stage choice is a sweet spot for the $N = 65536$ parameters — few enough levels to be affordable, and sparse enough that the rotation count per stage is still manageable.

Mod-eval

After coefficients-to-slots, the two slot-packed ciphertexts $\boxed{\cdot}_1$ and $\boxed{\cdot}_2$ sit at level $27$ and have slot values of the form

$$ \frac{m^\star_k}{q_0} \;=\; \frac{m_k}{q_0} \,+\, t_k, $$

where the index $k$ runs through bit-reversed even-then-odd coefficients of $m^\star$ as before, and where the coefficients-to-slots step folded a scale factor of $1/q_0$ into its output so that the slots contain $m^\star_k/q_0$ rather than $m^\star_k$. The integer part of each slot is $t_k \in \{-16, \ldots, 16\}$; the fractional part is $m_k/q_0$, the "useful" content. Applying the function

$$ x \;\longmapsto\; x \bmod 1 $$

slot-wise (where $x \bmod 1$ means $x$ minus its nearest integer) would strip off the $t_k$ and leave $m_k/q_0$ behind, which is exactly the coefficient we want back. Mod-eval is the homomorphic slot-wise evaluation of this function.

Choosing the mod-eval interval

The polynomial approximation of $x \bmod 1$ needs to hold on an interval $[-K,\, K]$ where $K$ is an upper bound on $\max_i |t_i|$. Smaller $K$ means lower polynomial degree, so we want $K$ as small as we can get away with.

We choose $K$ so that the per-bootstrap failure probability — the probability that some coefficient of $t$ falls outside $[-K, K]$ — is at most $2^{-128}$. There are $N = 65536$ coefficients, so by union bound it suffices to take $K$ with $\Pr[|t_i| > K] \le 2^{-128 - 16} = 2^{-144}$ for any single coefficient.

Near the upper edge of its support, the Irwin–Hall PDF behaves like $\bigl((h'+1)/2 - x\bigr)^{h'}/(h')!$, so the one-sided tail satisfies

$$ \Pr\bigl[\,t_i > K\,\bigr] \;\approx\; \frac{\bigl((h'+1)/2 - K\bigr)^{h' + 1}}{(h' + 1)!}. $$

Setting $h' = 32$ and $K = 16$, this gives

$$ \Pr\bigl[\,|t_i| > 16\,\bigr] \;\approx\; 2^{-155}, $$

per coefficient, so by union bound the probability that any coefficient of $t$ exceeds $16$ in magnitude is at most $2^{-155 + 16} = 2^{-139}$ — comfortably below $2^{-128}$. Setting $K = 15$ instead would give a per-coefficient bound of only $\approx 2^{-99}$, blowing the budget once unioned over $N$ coefficients.

So we'll take $K = 16$: the mod-eval polynomial needs to approximate $x \bmod 1$ accurately on $[-16, 16]$.

How accurate the approximation needs to be

The function $x \bmod 1$ isn't a polynomial — it's not even continuous — so it has to be replaced by a polynomial approximation. We have two parameters that control how hard the approximation has to work:

  • the size of the interval the approximation must cover, which we set to $[-K, K] = [-16, 16]$ above, and
  • the neighborhood radius $\delta$ around each integer in $[-K, K]$ within which the slot values actually live: by the previous derivation, slot values land at distance at most $\delta = \max_k |m_k|/q_0$ from the integer $t_k$.

So the polynomial doesn't need to approximate $x \bmod 1$ everywhere on $[-K, K]$ — only on the union of small neighborhoods

$$ I \;=\; \bigcup_{k=-K}^{K} \bigl[k - \delta,\, k + \delta\bigr] $$

around the integers. This is a much weaker requirement than uniform approximation on $[-K, K]$, and it's what lets a low-degree polynomial do the job. For the encryptions we care about, with $\Delta_0 \approx 2^{40}$ and message magnitudes bounded by $1$, $|m_k| \lesssim \Delta_0$, so $\delta \approx \Delta_0/q_0 \approx 2^{-15}$ — tiny.

The polynomial

On $I$, the function $x \bmod 1$ restricts to a smooth function: on each subinterval $[k - \delta, k + \delta]$ it's just $x - k$. The Remez algorithm (described in any approximation theory textbook) computes the unique polynomial of bounded degree that minimizes the worst-case error on a given target set, and for $I$ as above this produces a polynomial that approximates $x \bmod 1$ to error decaying exponentially in the degree.

For our parameters, a degree-$127$ Remez polynomial on $I$ achieves the precision we need. Evaluating a degree-$127$ polynomial homomorphically on the Chebyshev-like basis from Section 3.4 consumes $\lceil \log_2(127 + 1) \rceil = 7$ levels — matching the mod-eval budget.

Applying this polynomial slot-wise to $\boxed{\cdot}_1$ and $\boxed{\cdot}_2$ produces two level-$20$ ciphertexts whose slots hold $m_k/q_0$ for the bit-reversed even-then-odd indices $k$. That is, the slots now hold the rescaled coefficients of $m$, with the wrap-around term $t$ cleanly removed.

Slots-to-coefficients

The last step undoes coefficients-to-slots: it takes the two ciphertexts whose slots hold the rescaled coefficients of $m$ and produces a single ciphertext whose plaintext polynomial is (a scalar multiple of) $m$, encrypted at level $17$.

The construction mirrors coefficients-to-slots. With $\boxed{\cdot}_1$ and $\boxed{\cdot}_2$ holding the even- and odd-indexed bit-reversed coefficients of $m$, the combined ciphertext $\boxed{\cdot}_1 + i \cdot \boxed{\cdot}_2$ holds, in its slots, the values

$$ \bigl(m_{\mathrm{rev}(0)} + i\, m_{\mathrm{rev}(1)},\; \; m_{\mathrm{rev}(2)} + i\, m_{\mathrm{rev}(3)},\;\; \ldots,\;\; m_{\mathrm{rev}(N-2)} + i\, m_{\mathrm{rev}(N-1)}\bigr). $$

To turn this into a ciphertext whose plaintext polynomial has $(m_0, m_1, \ldots, m_{N-1})$ as coefficients, multiply slot-wise by the inverse of the coefficients-to-slots map:

$$ L^{-1} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(2)} & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(4)} & \cdots & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(N-2)} \\ 1 & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(2) \cdot 5} & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(4) \cdot 5} & \cdots & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(N-2) \cdot 5} \\ 1 & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(2) \cdot 25} & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(4) \cdot 25} & \cdots & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(N-2) \cdot 25} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 1 & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(2) \cdot 5^{N/2 - 1}} & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(4) \cdot 5^{N/2 - 1}} & \cdots & \zeta^{\mathrm{rev}(N-2) \cdot 5^{N/2 - 1}} \end{pmatrix}, $$

which is (up to a normalization) the inverse of the matrix $L$ from coefficients-to-slots. Applying $L^{-1}$ slot-wise to $\boxed{\cdot}_1 + i \cdot \boxed{\cdot}_2$ produces a ciphertext whose slots are the evaluations of $\sum_j m_j X^j$ at $X = \zeta, \zeta^5, \ldots$, which is precisely the slot representation of the plaintext polynomial $m$.

Sparse factorization of $L^{-1}$

Just like $L$, the inverse matrix $L^{-1}$ admits a factorization into $\log_2(N/2) = 15$ sparse matrices with three nonzero diagonals each. For the toy case $N = 16$, naming the three sparse factors $B_2$, $B_1$, $B_0$ (in order of application, right-to-left),

$$ L^{-1} \;=\; B_2 \cdot B_1 \cdot B_0, $$

where

$$ B_0 \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \zeta^4 & & & & & & \\ 1 & \zeta^{20} & & & & & & \\ & & 1 & \zeta^4 & & & & \\ & & 1 & \zeta^{20} & & & & \\ & & & & 1 & \zeta^4 & & \\ & & & & 1 & \zeta^{20} & & \\ & & & & & & 1 & \zeta^4 \\ & & & & & & 1 & \zeta^{20} \end{pmatrix}, $$ $$ B_1 \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & & \zeta^2 & & & & & \\ & 1 & & \zeta^{10} & & & & \\ 1 & & \zeta^{18} & & & & & \\ & 1 & & \zeta^{26} & & & & \\ & & & & 1 & & \zeta^2 & \\ & & & & & 1 & & \zeta^{10} \\ & & & & 1 & & \zeta^{18} & \\ & & & & & 1 & & \zeta^{26} \end{pmatrix}, $$ $$ B_2 \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & & & & \zeta & & & \\ & 1 & & & & \zeta^5 & & \\ & & 1 & & & & \zeta^{25} & \\ & & & 1 & & & & \zeta^{29} \\ 1 & & & & \zeta^{17} & & & \\ & 1 & & & & \zeta^{21} & & \\ & & 1 & & & & \zeta^9 & \\ & & & 1 & & & & \zeta^{13} \end{pmatrix}. $$

Multiplying out $B_2 \cdot B_1 \cdot B_0$ recovers

$$ L^{-1} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \zeta^4 & \zeta^2 & \zeta^6 & \zeta & \zeta^5 & \zeta^3 & \zeta^7 \\ 1 & \zeta^{20} & \zeta^{10} & \zeta^{30} & \zeta^5 & \zeta^{25} & \zeta^{15} & \zeta^3 \\ 1 & \zeta^4 & \zeta^{18} & \zeta^{22} & \zeta^{25} & \zeta^{29} & \zeta^{11} & \zeta^{15} \\ 1 & \zeta^{20} & \zeta^{26} & \zeta^{14} & \zeta^{29} & \zeta^{17} & \zeta^{23} & \zeta^{11} \\ 1 & \zeta^4 & \zeta^2 & \zeta^6 & \zeta^{17} & \zeta^{21} & \zeta^{19} & \zeta^{23} \\ 1 & \zeta^{20} & \zeta^{10} & \zeta^{30} & \zeta^{21} & \zeta^9 & \zeta^{31} & \zeta^{19} \\ 1 & \zeta^4 & \zeta^{18} & \zeta^{22} & \zeta^9 & \zeta^{13} & \zeta^{27} & \zeta^{31} \\ 1 & \zeta^{20} & \zeta^{26} & \zeta^{14} & \zeta^{13} & \zeta & \zeta^7 & \zeta^{27} \end{pmatrix}. $$

This is essentially the coefficients-to-slots factorization run in reverse: the diagonal offsets go $1 \to 2 \to 4$ (doubling), instead of $4 \to 2 \to 1$ (halving). For $N = 65536$, the full factorization again has $15$ sparse factors, which we combine into $3$ stages and apply via baby-step giant-step.

The output of this final step is a level-$17$ ciphertext encrypting the original vector $\mathbf{v}$, completing the bootstrap. The level has been refreshed from $0$ all the way back up to $17$, and the ciphertext is ready for $17$ more multiplications before it needs to be bootstrapped again.
